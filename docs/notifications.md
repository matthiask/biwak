# Notifications

Biwak tells you what happens to the things you follow, in the lantern
and by mail. You choose how much, and you can act on a mail without
opening Biwak.

## What you hear about

**Subscribed means you hear about everything on the item:** every
comment, change, completion. To stop hearing about one item,
unsubscribe from it.

You are subscribed to an item when you:

- create it, are assigned to it or comment on it;
- are mentioned in it (type `@` and a name in any text), unless your
  level is "Nothing";
- are in the project when somebody posts a message, doc or file, if
  your level is "Everything" or you're subscribed to its folder, or
  when they add you by name ("Also tell…");
- press "Subscribe" on it, or somebody adds you ("Subscribers..." in
  its "More" menu; they may tell you right away).

Subscribing to a list or a folder also tells you about what is created
in it later. Joining a project subscribes you to nothing that exists
already. "Unsubscribe" works on anything, except a to-do you are
assigned to: "Unassign me" instead. Every mail has a link to unsubscribe
from its item.

You are never told about what you did yourself, and only about what you
can see.

## Levels

Levels decide what you are subscribed to automatically, and whether a
new to-do in a list you follow reaches you. In **Settings**, under
Notifications, "Notify me about" is one of:

- **Everything** (the default): posts to everybody subscribe you, and
  you hear about everything new in the lists you follow;
- **Only what concerns me:** only being mentioned or assigned and your
  own things subscribe you;
- **Nothing:** only what you do yourself (commenting, being assigned)
  subscribes you; mentions don't reach you. The activity feed still
  shows everything.

A project can have its own level ("More", then "Notifications" on the
project's page), and so can a list ("Notifications..." in its "More"
menu): the closest one wins. Other items have no level: subscribe or
unsubscribe. On a project's people page staff see who is "Notified by
default" and who is notified "Only when tagged", and can change it;
clients and partners change only their own. Whoever adds you picks the first setting.

## The lantern

The lamp icon in the bar shows a dot while something is unread. Opening
it lists the unread entries grouped by item, newest first, then the
latest read ones. The page "All notifications" has the whole list with
"Unread" and "Mentions only".

- **Opening an entry reads it** (with the item's comments) and takes you
  to the item, at the comment where it applies.
- **Mark all as read**, or mark one item as read or unread.
- **Acting on an item reads it:** completing or reopening a to-do,
  changing it, ticking its checklist, commenting, reacting, or
  answering a check-in. Only looking doesn't: opening an item from the
  feed, a list, search or a mail leaves it unread. Bookmarking and
  subscribing don't count either.
- Bookmarked items with notifications stay in a "Bookmarked" section.
- Rows on Home with unread notifications are marked and read in the
  same way.
- Settings has an option for a dot on the browser tab's icon.

## Mails

Under Settings, "Mails":

- **A mail for each change** (the default): one mail per item, sent
  about ten minutes after its last change, so quick edits and several
  comments arrive together;
- **A daily digest** at 07:00, with what happened the day before;
- **No mails:** the lantern only, also no check-in questions and
  summaries.

What you have read in the lantern before the mail goes out isn't mailed,
and a mail with nothing left isn't sent. Mails carry the full text; the
first line says when clients can see the item.

**When mail bounces.** If mail to someone's address is rejected for good
or marked as spam, Biwak stops sending them notification mails, digests
and check-in questions; what happens stays in their lantern. Their
activity page says so (to staff and to themselves), and so does their
settings page. It starts again once an admin changes the address or
clears it in the admin (People, "Mail to the selected people works
again"); what happened in between isn't mailed afterwards, it's in the
lantern.

## Replying by mail

When a mail ends with "Reply to this mail to comment." or "Reply to
this mail to answer.", your reply is posted as your comment, or as your
answer to a check-in. Reply from the address the mail was sent to.
Quoted text and signatures are cut off. If a reply can't be posted
(another address, an expired reply address, you can't see the item any
more) you get one short mail saying why. A mail's reply address works
for six months. Replying works only where the installation has it
switched on.
