# Keyboard

You can do most of what you do in Biwak without the mouse: a command
palette to jump anywhere and run actions, and single-key shortcuts
inside pages, like GitHub's.

## The command palette

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac) or
<kbd>/</kbd> opens it. The search button in the bar does the same.

- **Empty:** the actions of the page you are on, then your bookmarks, then
  the pages you visited last in this browser.
- **Typing** filters the actions and looks for projects, lists, messages,
  docs, files, folders, check-ins and people that match. You only find
  what you may see.
- <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> move, <kbd>Enter</kbd> runs,
  <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes. "Search for ..." at the end opens the search
  page with your text.
- **Actions** depend on the page and on what you may do: everywhere
  "Go home", "Go to projects", "Go to the activity", "Go to to-dos",
  "To-dos I assigned", "Go to bookmarks", "Go to people", "Settings",
  "Search", "Help", "Keyboard shortcuts", "Switch the theme"; in a project "New message",
  "New doc" and so on; on a to-do "Complete", "Comment", "Edit",
  "Bookmark", "Subscribe", "Copy the link".

Inside a Markdown text field <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> inserts a
link instead. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> first or click outside the field to
reach the palette.

## Shortcuts in pages

- <kbd>j</kbd> and <kbd>k</kbd> move through the rows of a page: to-dos
  on lists, the board and the to-dos page, messages, search results,
  entries in the activity feed. The current row is outlined.
- <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>o</kbd> opens the current row.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>x</kbd> completes or reopens the current to-do,
  or the to-do whose page you are on.
- <kbd>x</kbd> selects the current to-do (on a list, the board and the
  to-dos page) or a folder's tile, or takes it out of the selection;
  <kbd>Shift</kbd> with the arrow keys selects on. See
  [Assigning several at once](todos.md#assigning-several-at-once).
- <kbd>c</kbd> writes a comment, <kbd>e</kbd> edits, <kbd>b</kbd>
  bookmarks, <kbd>s</kbd> subscribes or unsubscribes, on an item's page.
- <kbd>g</kbd>, then a letter, goes somewhere: <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>h</kbd>
  Home, <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>p</kbd> projects, <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>t</kbd> your
  to-dos, <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>f</kbd> the activity, <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>b</kbd>
  bookmarks. Press the second key within a second.
- <kbd>?</kbd> opens a list of the shortcuts of the page you are on and
  of the shortcuts that work everywhere.

Shortcuts never fire while you type in a field, with
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> held, or while a dialog
is open.

## Turning them off

Single keys can get in the way of screen readers and voice control. In
**Settings**, under Keyboard, you can turn the single-key shortcuts
off. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> keeps working. <kbd>/</kbd> is a
single key and goes with the setting. Selecting with <kbd>x</kbd> on a to-do or a tile
that has the focus (after <kbd>Tab</kbd>) keeps working.
